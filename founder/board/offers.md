# Offers lens
Score: 4/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **The market is reachable.** Women 50+ in the US have money and can be found on Facebook and Instagram. The three pains named in the brief (no color suits me, lipstick dries the lips, color bleeds into lines) are specific and easy to say in an ad.
- **Low effort, fast result.** It is a balm: swipe it on and see color in minutes. Effort and time to result, two parts of the value equation, are already in the buyer's favor.
- **The guarantee is the one real edge.** A 365-day money-back guarantee that covers opened product is far stronger than the category leader's 90-day guarantee, which excludes opened product. Of the "honest" points in the brief, this is the only one no checked competitor already offers.
- **Fit with the brand.** "Your skin was never the problem" matches "no color suits me" well. A later bundle with the Complexion Stick gives the stack somewhere to grow.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **No proof, so likelihood is near zero.** No samples, no factory, and the hydration claim is untested. The color is the same single Red 27 pink every competitor uses, so "suits you" cannot rest on the chemistry. Honest claims, no subscription and visible color hours are already used by Olmera and Nektar. Without tested hydration and real before/after proof, this is a commodity with nicer copy.
2. **Price with nothing stacked to justify it.** $36 for one balm sits against buy-1-get-1 offers that work out to about $20 a unit (Kivora) or about $10 a unit (Areliaa). The 2-pack at $59 is still about $29.50 a unit. Today the offer is "the same pink, more honest, costs more". The lens asks what would make saying no feel stupid, and the brief does not answer that yet.
3. **The unit economics are unknown and the guarantee could drain cash.** Unit cost ($5) and shipping (~$5) are estimates, and the factory's minimum order is unknown. Payment fees, cost per purchase on Meta and the refund rate are also unknown. A guarantee that covers opened product invites refunds in a category with weak-color complaints. On a budget under $1,500, one bad factory order or a high refund rate ends the test.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Once samples arrive, how will you prove hydration? For example, a wear test with a set number of women 50+ over a set number of days, with their own words and photos, collected before launch.
- Does a sheer balm actually solve "bleeds into the lines"? If not, which of the three pains does the offer lead with?
- What would buyers get beyond the balm that a $10 BOGO cannot match? Possible additions: a lip prep routine, a guide to wearing color after 50, a mini of the Complexion Stick.
- What is the target cost per purchase, and the most you can pay and still profit at $36 after unit cost, shipping, fees and an expected refund rate?
- What refund rate can the 365-day opened-product guarantee absorb before it loses money? Will you cap it at one claim per customer?
- Could you double the price, or hold it at $36, and still be the obvious choice? What would have to be true?
- What is the factory's minimum order, and does it fit inside $1,500 alongside samples, content and test ads?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. Samples are tested on real women 50+ before any ad spend, and the hydration claim survives. If it fails, drop the claim or the product.
2. A one-page break-even sheet built from real factory quotes: unit cost, shipping, fees, refund-rate assumption and the highest cost per purchase you can afford at $36 and at $59.
3. An offer stack the BOGO brands do not have, built from the buyer's problems on the way to "a color that suits me". The guarantee is the headline, with at least one cheap, high-value addition and a clear offer name. All of it must be honest: no fake scarcity, counters or reviews.
4. A written refund policy that makes the guarantee clear and bounded (one claim per customer, simple process), plus a stop-loss number for the ad test.
