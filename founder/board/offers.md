# Offers lens
Score: 5/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **Reachable buyers with money.** UK women 45–65 can be targeted on Meta, and the early numbers show it: 43 waitlist sign-ups at about £4–5 each on the best ad's recent days and £7–8 over its life.
- **Low effort for the buyer.** Two gummies a day, berry flavour, no water or capsules. On the value equation, effort and sacrifice are close to zero.
- **A guarantee already exists.** "Finish your first jar or get a full refund" is a real start at risk reversal.
- **Bundle pricing is shaped well.** 3 jars for the price of 2 single jars, free delivery from 2 jars, and profit rising from about £15 to £22.20 per order makes the bigger basket the obvious pick.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **It is a commodity, sold on a thin difference.** The base product is white-label and other brands can buy the same thing. "The dose printed on the front" is a label choice a rival can copy in a week. With no outcome-based reason to choose Mëva, the only lever left is price, and this lens says never compete there.
2. **Weak dream outcome and no proof of likelihood.** Claims are limited to the five authorised magnesium claims (tiredness, muscle, psychological function), with no menopause or hormone language allowed. There are no reviews, no customers and no stated time to feel a result. Buyers are asked to pay £24.99 on faith.
3. **The numbers may not close.** Profit before ads is about £15 on 1 jar and about £11 with the Founding discount. At £7.50 per sign-up, break-even needs about half of sign-ups to buy a full-price jar, or about two-thirds at the Founding price. Nobody yet knows the real conversion rate. The "Founding 500" cap is also not real scarcity when there are 43 sign-ups.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- What share of the waitlist actually buys, and what does a purchase from a cold ad cost?
- How fast does a buyer notice anything, and what can you honestly promise in the first 30 days?
- What stops another brand on the same supplier from selling this exact gummy for less next month?
- What does the buyer struggle with on the way to feeling better (sleep routine, remembering the dose, knowing it works), and which of those could you solve cheaply as part of the offer?
- Could you charge more and still be the obvious choice? If not, why not?
- What is the repeat-purchase rate, given there is no subscription?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. Measure conversion from waitlist to purchase and the cost per purchase from cold ads in the first sales window. Show that a first order covers its ad cost, or that 2- and 3-jar orders get it there.
2. Build a stack around the outcome, not the label: for example, a 30-day routine or tracker, a clearer guarantee headline, and a reorder or subscribe option. Keep only the parts that are cheap to deliver.
3. Replace the 500 cap with honest urgency tied to the real first stock batch (50 jars), or drop it.
4. Collect reviews and before/after self-reports from the first buyers so the next ad can show proof.
