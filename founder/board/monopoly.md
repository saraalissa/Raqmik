# Monopoly lens

Score: 3/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- There is a small market to aim at: US women 50+ who quit lipstick because of dryness, bleeding into lip lines, or "no color suits me". It is narrower than the general pH lipstick buyer the 14+ copy brands chase with advertorials.
- A brand foothold already exists. MAHSA already sells to that same woman ("Your skin was never the problem"), and a second product can share the customer list, the brand, and later a bundle with the Complexion Stick. A brand is the only one of the four moats this business can realistically build.
- The weak spots of the competition are documented and dated (2026-11-26). The leader shows 3.8/5 on Trustpilot while claiming 4.6-4.8 on its own site, lists no full ingredient list, and excludes opened product from its guarantee. One brand hides a monthly subscription. Trust is visibly broken in this category.
- The cost of testing is small (under $1,500), so being wrong costs little.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **Nothing here is proprietary and every edge can be copied fast.** The core is the same Red 27 (CI 45410) chemistry everyone uses, made by a contract manufacturer anyone can hire. "Honest claims" already exists (Olmera at $9.99 names Red 27 and states 4-6 hours of color). "No subscription, ever" already exists (Nektar). Full INCI and a longer guarantee can be copied by a rival in weeks. This is an incremental improvement in a crowded market, which is where margins go to zero.
2. **Distribution is rented and unpriced.** The only planned channel is Meta ads, the same channel 14+ competitors bid on for the same buyer, some at $9.99-$19.97 with buy-1-get-1. Cost to acquire a customer is unknown, so it is unknown whether the first 100 customers can be bought profitably at ~$36 with a ~$5 unit cost and ~$5 shipping (both still estimates).
3. **The one thing that could be "10x better" is not proven.** Real hydration for dry, lined 50+ lips is the only claim that could separate this from the pack, and it is untested: no samples, no factory chosen. If it is not clearly better, the product is a premium-priced copy, and the 365-day guarantee on opened product turns from a trust signal into a refund liability.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- What is the secret: what do you know about this buyer that the advertorial brands do not, and that they could not learn by reading your site?
- What stops a copycat in month six from adding a full INCI, a 365-day guarantee, and "no subscription" at $19.99?
- What is the smallest market you could own first (for example, existing Complexion Stick buyers), and what share of it could you realistically reach?
- How do the first 100 customers arrive, and what does each one cost? What CAC breaks the unit economics at $34-$39?
- Is the hydration measurably better than Areliaa's (whose buyers complain about reapplying on dry lips), and how will you show it without anti-aging claims?
- Why now? What changed that gives this an opening today rather than a year ago or a year from now?
- What is the refund rate you can survive on a guarantee that covers opened product, and what do you expect it to be?
- In ten years, what does MAHSA own that a new entrant cannot buy: the customer relationship, the brand, a formula, a community?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. **Prove the one better thing first.** Before any ad spend beyond a small test, get samples and show the hydration is clearly better on dry, lined lips (a documented wear test with real 50+ users, results stated honestly). If it is not, stop: there is no product edge to build on.
2. **Start with the market you already own.** Sell first to existing MAHSA / Complexion Stick customers and their circle, and report the conversion and repeat rate. Owning a small group fully beats a small share of the crowded Meta auction.
3. **Price the channel before scaling it.** Run the ABO test and report actual cost per purchase against contribution margin once unit cost, shipping and refund rate are real numbers, not estimates. Scale only if a customer pays back on the first order or on a clear second purchase (bundle).
4. **Name the durable asset.** Show a written plan for what accumulates and cannot be copied: the brand and story for this one buyer, an owned audience (email/SMS list, community), and published wear-test proof. Honesty alone is a feature rivals can copy; a trusted brand with an owned audience is the only moat in reach.
