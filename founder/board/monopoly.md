# Monopoly lens
Score: 3/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **Cheap to test.** First stock is 50 jars (about £208 ex VAT), repeat orders take about 5 working days, and fixed costs are low. Finding out costs little.
- **Healthy gross margin before ads.** About £15 profit on 1 jar and £22.20 on 3 jars leaves room to pay for customers, if cost per purchase comes in low enough.
- **A tight first segment.** UK women 45–65 on Meta is a small market the business can name and reach. That is the right way to start under this lens.
- **Some distribution signal.** 43 waitlist sign-ups in 8 days, with the best ad at about £4–5 per sign-up recently, shows people will respond to the ads. It does not yet show they will buy.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **Nothing is proprietary.** It is a white-label stock product that "other brands can buy". There is no technology, no know-how and no economies of scale. Printing the dose on the front of the label is a message, and a rival can copy it in a week. A copycat in month six costs almost nothing.
2. **The market is probably crowded, but nobody has checked.** Magnesium gummies are a common supplement format, and competitors have not been researched. If the same buyer sees many similar jars, prices fall towards cost, which is exactly what this lens warns about.
3. **Distribution is rented, not owned.** Every customer so far comes from Meta. Sign-ups cost £4–25 each, and real cost per purchase is unknown. If a 1-jar order makes about £15 (about £11 with the Founding discount), a cost per purchase above that loses money on the first order. The business then depends on repeat purchases that have not been measured and on one ad platform's prices.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Who else sells magnesium gummies to UK women 45–65 on Instagram, and at what price? (Open question.)
- What is the secret: what do you know about this buyer that the other brands do not?
- What stops your own supplier's other customers from making the same claim on the same product?
- What is the real cost per first purchase from cold ads, and what is the repeat rate? (Both open questions.)
- Which durable advantage could you build within 12 months: your own formulation, an owned audience (email list, community), or a brand strong enough to command a premium?
- Why now? What has changed that makes this the moment?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. **A competitor map before launch.** List the direct UK competitors, their prices and their claims, and show the gap Mëva can own.
2. **Proof of distribution economics.** Over the first launch window, measure cost per purchase against first-order profit and the repeat rate. Continue only if a customer pays back within a number of orders the founder sets in advance.
3. **A plan for durability.** Name one advantage to build (an owned customer list with real engagement, a differentiated formulation, or a niche positioning that others cannot claim) and the milestone that proves it.
4. **Basic risk covered.** Put product liability insurance in place before the first sale.
