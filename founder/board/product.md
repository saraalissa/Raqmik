# Product lens
Score: 5/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **Focus is real.** One SKU, one flavour, one dose, one market (GB women 45–65), one channel. Nothing to cut from the range.
- **It fits in one sentence:** "Magnesium gummies for women 45–65, with the dose printed on the front." That is rare at this stage.
- **The product is easy to recognise.** A clear jar, a silver lid, a coral label and glossy deep-purple pyramids are distinctive in a category full of white tubs. The craft shows in the visual standard the brand already holds itself to.
- **The claims are clean.** It sticks to the five authorised claims and makes no menopause promises. That protects trust, which is the whole positioning.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **The product inside is not owned.** The base gummy is white-label, and any brand can buy the same one. "Dose on the front" is a label choice, and a competitor can copy it in a week. If the only thing Mëva owns is the packaging, the experience after the jar is opened has to carry the brand. Nothing in the brief says what that experience is.
2. **The first five minutes, and the first 30 days, may feel like nothing.** Magnesium does not give a felt result on day one, and the guarantee asks the buyer to finish the jar. The brief has no designed moment that tells her it is working: no unboxing, no day-7 check-in, no routine cue. Without one, repeat rate (unknown) is the number that decides the business.
3. **The experience has many seams and one person.** A solo founder outside the UK runs a Spanish manufacturer, a UK packer, a 3PL, Shopify, Meta and email. VAT is pending and there is no product liability insurance yet. The touchpoint that can be measured is already weak: the welcome email gets a 3% click rate. Seams are where the experience breaks, and here nobody is on the ground to catch it.

Also worth a look: the gummies contain sugar and glucose syrup. For a brand built on "nothing hidden", the front of the jar should not leave her surprised by the back.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Describe the moment she opens the parcel. What does she see, read and do in the first five minutes?
- What tells her on day 7 and day 21 that it is worth carrying on?
- What could Mëva own that a brand buying the same gummy cannot copy?
- Who checks a damaged parcel, a wrong order or a late delivery, and how fast?
- Why "Founding 500" when the list has 43 people? Does the name promise more than is true?
- How is the sugar content shown, and has a buyer reacted to it?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. Product liability insurance and VAT registration in place before the first sale.
2. A written, designed post-purchase journey (unboxing insert, day-7 and day-21 emails) live at launch, and a welcome email rewritten and retested.
3. A test order sent through the full chain (supplier, packer, 3PL, doorstep) and checked against the master product image before sales open.
4. Repeat purchase rate measured on the first cohort before ad spend is scaled; the target is an open question the founder must set.
