# Product lens
Score: 4/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- The customer moment is clear and specific: a woman 50+ who stopped wearing lipstick because no color suits her, it dries her lips, or it bleeds into the lines around her mouth. Three concrete failures make a good spec to design against.
- The brand already exists and has one idea ("Your skin was never the problem"), so the balm can be its second chapter, not a separate product.
- The planned touchpoints are honest: full INCI, no subscription, a guarantee that covers opened product, no fake counters or reviews. In this category the competitors break at exactly these points (hidden subscriptions, guarantees that exclude opened product, site ratings above Trustpilot), so owning them is part of the product.
- The founder knows the chemistry (Red 27 gives the same cool pink on everyone) and chose not to repeat the "unique shade for you" claim.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **The product itself does not exist yet.** There are no samples, no chosen factory and no hydration test. The only edge that lives in the jar ("real hydration") is unproven. Everything else on the list (INCI, guarantee, no subscription) is policy, which a competitor can copy within a week. If the balm in the tube feels like the $9.99 version, the honest wrapping will not save it.
2. **The first five minutes may disappoint the exact buyer it targets.** She says "no color suits me". Red 27 turns the same single cool pink on everyone. If that pink does not suit her, the product repeats her original problem on day one. It is not known whether a sheer balm stops bleeding into the lines around the mouth or simply bleeds less visibly. It is also not known how it behaves on dry, mature lips, which is the reapplication complaint competitors already get.
3. **No focus at launch.** One balm, a 2-pack through a bundle app, a later Complexion Stick bundle, three ad angles, UGC, and a 365-day opened-product guarantee, all on under $1,500. Each extra piece is another seam (bundle app, refund handling, manufacturer, shipping) where the experience can break before the core product is proven.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- In one sentence, what does this balm do that the buyer cannot get from the $9.99 or $19.97 version?
- Walk me through the first five minutes: she opens the package, swipes it on, looks in the mirror. What does she see at minute one and at hour four? Have you seen it on a 50+ face yet?
- What will you test on samples before you order: hydration over what time, feathering on lined lips, color on different lip tones? Who tests it and what counts as a pass?
- If the cool pink does not flatter her, what happens? Is the honest answer "it is a moisturizing balm with a hint of pink", and is that enough?
- What would you cut from the launch so that only the single balm, one ad angle, and one clean checkout remain?
- How does the opened-product guarantee work in practice: does she ship it back, is it refunded without return, and who handles it? What does that cost per claim? (Unknown today.)
- What is the real unit cost once a factory is chosen? The $5 figure is an estimate.

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. Samples from a chosen manufacturer are tested on women 50+ before any order, with a written pass bar on hydration duration, feathering, and whether the pink is wearable. If it fails, do not launch.
2. Launch with one SKU and one sentence. Leave out the 2-pack, the bundle app and the Complexion Stick bundle until the single balm has repeat buyers or low return rates.
3. The one-sentence claim is written from the sample results and nothing else (for example, what it actually does to dry lips and how long), and the ad, product page, package and follow-up email all say the same thing.
4. The guarantee and return flow is designed and costed end to end before the first ad runs.
