**Bottom line:** The dye is Red 27 / CI 45410. The color is NOT truly unique per person: moisture (not pH) is the main trigger, lip pH (~5) is always above the dye's ~pH 4.0 switch point, so everyone gets the same single cool pink. ## The 5 strongest gaps MAHSA can honestly use (ranked by evidence strength)

1. **A guarantee that actually covers a tried product.** Kivora markets a "90-day money-back guarantee" while its binding policy limits returns to "unused, unopened, with the hygiene seal intact" — open it and you're excluded. Nektar promises 90 days in its badge and 30 days in its own body text; Relvie badges 90 days while its returns text says 60; Lumi & Luna is unopened-only. MAHSA can state one window, once, and honor it for opened product. (Evidence: 4 opened policy/product pages with verbatim contradictions.)
2. **Numbers that add up.** Areliaa claims 100,000+ customers on one live page and 200,000 on another; Kivora claims 4.6/5 and 4.8/5 on its own site against Trustpilot's actual 3.8/5 from 1,283 reviews; Relvie shows three different customer counts (30,000+ / 100,000+ / 127,000); Lumi & Luna shows 2,489 and 14,300 reviews on the same page. MAHSA: one review count, one rating, consistent everywhere, matching its third-party profile. (Evidence: same-day, same-brand page pairs, all opened.)
3. **Kill the "unique shade created just for you" myth — honestly.** Lab Muffin (PhD cosmetic chemist): Red 27 "creates just the one pink shade"; the trigger is moisture, and everyone's lip pH sits above the switch point. The honest version — "a sheer pink stain that lets your own lip color show through, deeper the more you layer" — is exactly what the chemistry supports, and no major rival says it straight. (Evidence: Lab Muffin + The List/Allure chemist + Science Focus, plus five rivals' INCI lists naming CI 45410.)
4. **Publish the full ingredient list.** Kivora — the category's hub brand — shows NO INCI list on its product page at all; only three marketing ingredients in an FAQ. Areliaa's own customers voice ingredient-safety unease on Trustpilot ("Feel like need to research every ingredient to check its toxicity"), and Areliaa confirms China manufacture in its replies. MAHSA: full INCI on the product page, dye named, origin stated. (Evidence: Kivora page searched — no "Ingredients"; Areliaa Trustpilot quotes.)
5. **No subscription traps, stated plainly.** Fenvio auto-adds a "VIP" pass that converts to $29.95/month on day 15 — its Trustpilot page (4.4, 2,560 reviews) is dominated by billing complaints, including an 85-year-old charged for 7 months with no product sent. Kivora and Relvie quietly enroll buyers in 4-week refills via FAQ text. Nektar already markets "One-time purchase — no subscription… ever" as a headline — proof the counter-position sells. MAHSA: one-time purchase, no auto-refill, said on the product page, not in an FAQ. (Evidence: verbatim Trustpilot complaints + opened FAQ/offer text.)

*Honorable mention:* performance honesty — Areliaa reviewers flag reapplication on dry lips and weak color; IPSY's 3 testers got zero strong first-application changes from Dear Midnight; Winky Lux's documented flaw is breakage, not dryness. A balm that leads with real hydration and realistic wear time ("4–6 hours", as Olmera — the cheapest brand — already admits) owns the honest lane at a premium price.
