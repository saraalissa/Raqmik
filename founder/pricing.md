# Pricing: Mëva (22 Nov 2026)

Inputs: 20 simulated buyers (pricing-curve.md), competitors.md, unit costs from board/brief.md (product £4.16/jar incl. label, 3PL £4.84 / £5.29 / £5.70 per 1/2/3-jar order, card fees ~3.5% of gross, 20% VAT). Profits below are BEFORE advertising.

## What buyers said (simulated, pick-what-to-test only)
Acceptable range £9.04–£25.00 · least resistance (OPP) £12.08 · indifference (IPP) £15.04 · PME £25.00. Median: bargain £14, getting expensive £22, too expensive £30. **£24.99 for one jar sits at the very top edge.**

## What competitors charge (30 days, gummies)
£16.99–£29.99, median ~£19.49. Same dose/form as Mëva: Novomins £19.99 (£14.99 subscribe), Unfabled £16.99. Tablets £5–9.

## 1. The price: keep the ladder, change how it is shown
- 1 jar £24.99 stays as the **trial rung** (with the guarantee). It is a real price, charged, not an anchor.
- **2 jars £39.98 = £19.99 a jar**: the same as Novomins' one-off price, plus free delivery. Make it the default ("Our pick").
- **3 jars £49.98 = £16.66 a jar**: cheaper per jar than every gummy competitor.
- Show **per jar and per day** on every pack (83p / 67p / 56p a day). This answers the "£25 vs Boots/Novomins" objection with facts.
Why not cut the single jar: it would cost £2.40+ per order and still not beat £5 tablets; the battle is won on the 2-jar price, which already matches the market.

## 2. The ladder (reason to step up)
1 jar: try it (guarantee) → 2 jars: £19.99 a jar + free delivery (no £4.99 fee, the panel's delivery objection) → 3 jars: lowest price per jar, a full 90-day routine.
Later (after launch, if repeat is good): an optional reorder reminder or subscribe-and-save, clearly cancellable (competitors' biggest brand complaint is subscription billing).

## 3. Opening offer (Founding)
- Keep 20% off the first order, but **with a real end date** (e.g. 7 days after launch or when the first batch sells out), not "first 500" (we have ~43 sign-ups; the board flagged it as false scarcity).
- Apply it to **any pack**, so founders are pushed to 2 jars: 2 jars £31.98 → profit ≈ £11.90; 1 jar £19.99 + £4.99 → profit £10.94. Needs Sara's decision (today it applies to 1 jar only).

## 4. What to test with real buyers
- Pack mix at launch: share of orders on 1 vs 2 vs 3 jars with "2 jars" pre-selected vs "1 jar" pre-selected (two product-page versions, split by ad set).
- Single jar £24.99 vs £21.99 only if 1-jar orders dominate and convert poorly.
- Real price survey: add the 4 Van Westendorp questions to the welcome/launch email for real answers.

## 5. Top price objections (simulated, verbatim)
- "Why would I pay five times the price of a supermarket or Boots magnesium for something that is mostly sugar"
- "£24.99 for 30 days of gummies is a lot to pay for something I haven't tried"
- "£4.99 delivery on one jar, and I'm not convinced gummies are a serious supplement"

## Warning for the CFO
At £5–7.50 per waitlist sign-up and an unknown waitlist→purchase rate (say 15%), cost per buyer could be £33–50, above first-order profit (£11–22). The business works only if most buyers take 2–3 jars and reorder. Next: /founder-offer, then /founder-cfo.
