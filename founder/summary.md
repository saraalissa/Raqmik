**What it is.** Mëva sells glossy magnesium berry gummies (180mg magnesium citrate in 2 a day, dose printed on the front) online to UK women aged 45–65. It sells only through trymeva.co, with Meta ads, as a one-off purchase with no subscription.

**The three numbers (base case, estimates):**
- Each order leaves **£16.42 before ads**. After a blended ad cost of £21 per order (£30 per new buyer, 30% repeat orders) it **loses £4.58** (−14%).
- Break-even: **never**, at that ad cost. Every order loses money.
- Year 1: **−£8,224** operating (−£10,282 after the £2,058 startup).
- At £20 per new buyer year 1 is +£1,226. At £12 it is +£8,786, and the startup is earned back in month 6.

**Panel and board.**
- Agreed: the product and the transparency angle are sound. Price against £5 tablets, trust in a brand with no reviews, and sugar are the blockers.
- The board voted 3× fund if (avg 4.3/10). Its conditions are proof that buyers pay and that cost per buyer is below first-order profit.
- The panel (20 simulated buyers) bought 0 times, even with the improved offer. 19 of 20 would join the waitlist.
- Where they disagree: the board sees a business if the conditions are met; the panel says nobody buys an unreviewed brand before launch. The real waitlist will settle it.

**Biggest risk:** cold Meta ads cost more per buyer than an order earns.
