# Board summary: Mëva (22 Nov 2026)

**Vote: 3 × FUND IF. Average score 4.3/10** (Offers 5, Product 5, Monopoly 3).

## Risks raised by more than one member (most important first)
1. **Commodity product.** White-label stock gummy any brand can buy; "dose on the front" is easy to copy (all three).
2. **Unproven economics.** Profit before ads £15 (1 jar) / £11 (Founding price). At £7.50 per waitlist sign-up, about 50% of sign-ups must buy at full price (68% at Founding price) just to break even on the first order. Waitlist→purchase rate, cost per purchase and repeat rate are unknown (Offers, Monopoly, Product).
3. **Weak felt result and no proof yet.** Only the 5 authorised claims, slow effect, no reviews; repeat rate decides the business (Offers, Product). The 20-buyer panel said the same: 0 of 20 would buy now; 16 would join the waitlist.
4. **"Founding 500" is not real scarcity** with 43 sign-ups (Offers, Product).
5. **Operational and legal gaps** before the first sale: product liability insurance, VAT number, many handoffs for a solo founder (Monopoly, Product).

## Conditions checklist
- [ ] Product liability insurance in place before the first sale
- [ ] UK VAT registration handled (sales can start before the number per gov.uk, but VAT must be accounted for)
- [ ] Measure waitlist→purchase conversion and cost per purchase from cold ads; first order should cover its ad cost, or a payback limit set in advance
- [ ] Measure repeat purchase on the first buyers before raising ad spend (founder sets the target)
- [ ] Map UK competitors (prices, claims) — /founder-competitors
- [ ] Name one durable advantage and the milestone that proves it (owned list, different formulation, a niche)
- [ ] Rebuild the offer around the result: 30-day routine/tracker, stronger guarantee headline, reorder or subscription option
- [ ] Replace the 500 cap with honest urgency tied to the first 50-jar batch
- [ ] Post-purchase experience live at launch: leaflet, emails on day 7 and day 21; collect reviews from first buyers
- [ ] One full test order end to end before sales open

## Disagreements kept
- Monopoly scores it lowest (3): without a proprietary product there is no long-term business. Offers and Product (5) see a workable small test if the offer and the first-30-days experience are fixed.

## The strongest version the board can see
Not "another magnesium gummy", but the UK's most transparent midlife magnesium routine: a 30-day programme (routine, tracker, check-in emails, honest guarantee) sold as a 2–3 jar starter, with reviews from the first 50 buyers as proof. Treat the first 50 jars as a paid test of conversion and repeat, not a launch; only scale ads once one order reliably pays back its ad cost. Long term, the moat is the owned customer list and a formulation or niche that is not a stock item. This differs from the current pitch, which sells a single jar on price and label transparency alone.
