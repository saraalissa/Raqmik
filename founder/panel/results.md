## What would flip a no

- Free delivery on a single jar, plus proof from someone like a pharmacist that the gummies give the same magnesium as a tablet.
- Clear per-gummy sugar numbers and real reviews from women my age who've taken it for a few weeks, plus the Founding 500 discount when it launches.
- Hearing from someone my age, or from my pharmacist or GP, that it has helped with tiredness and that the amount is right for me.
- Hearing from a woman my age, in a similar job, that the gummies taste clean, aren't sticky or overly sweet, and that the magnesium really helped her tiredness.
- A first jar at a clearly lower price, or an independent test showing the magnesium in the jar matches the label.
- A sugar-free version, or a clear reason to think the sugar doesn't matter much at two a day.
- A low-sugar or sugar-free version, or a clear grams-of-sugar figure per gummy that I can compare with my current magnesium.
- A launch price much closer to the Boots price, or proof that this form of magnesium works better than the cheap tablets I can already buy.
- Independent reviews or a friend I trust saying they've felt a real difference, plus a price nearer the Boots one.
- An independent lab test, or a friend I trust telling me it actually helped her tiredness, plus a price that comes down near the Boots price.
- A clear, independent answer that magnesium really helps tiredness in women my age, plus a price I can justify against what I already spend.
- Real reviews from women my age in the UK who say they noticed a difference after a few weeks, and a proper way to see who is behind the brand.
