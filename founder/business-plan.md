## The idea

- What it is: a direct-to-consumer magnesium gummy brand for UK women in midlife, sold online.
- Who it is for: women aged 45–65 in England, Scotland and Wales who care about their health, shop on their phone and buy supplements or self-care products several times a year. Main motives: tiredness and fatigue, nerves and mood, muscle tension.
- What it sells, at what price: Mëva Magnesium Berry Gummies, 60 gummies per jar (30 days). 2 gummies a day = 180mg magnesium (citrate), 48% NRV; pectin based (vegan), contains sugar and glucose syrup. 1 jar £24.99, 2 jars £39.98, 3 jars £49.98. One-off purchase, no subscription.
- Where and how: only on trymeva.co (Shopify), customers come from Meta ads (Instagram and Facebook). Card payment online. Delivery in Great Britain in 2–3 working days via a UK 3PL, £4.99 or free over £29.
- Budget and constraints: solo founder, run through a US company (Organo LLC) with no UK establishment. Small budget (first stock order 50 jars, about £208 ex VAT). Product is a white-label stock item from a UK supplier (Nutribl, made in Spain, packed in the UK). Pre-launch: UK VAT number applied for, labelling partner (food business operator, £395/year) being contracted. Only the 5 GB-authorised magnesium health claims may be used.

## The numbers

- **The margin.** One blended order (30% 1 jar / 50% 2 jars / 20% 3 jars) brings £32.48 ex VAT. After product, 3PL, card fees and the guarantees it leaves **£16.42 before ads**. That £16.42 is the most Meta can cost per order.
- **The line to watch: cost per buyer (ads).** Everything else is small (fixed costs only £170/month). Blended ad cost per order (assumes 30% of year-1 orders are repeats with no ad cost):
  | cost per NEW buyer | waitlist→buyer at £6 per sign-up | contribution per order | year 1 |
  | --- | --- | ---: | ---: |
  | £12 | 50% | +£8.02 | +£8,786 |
  | £20 | 30% | +£2.42 | +£1,226 |
  | £30 | 20% | −£4.58 | −£8,224 |
  | £40 | 15% | −£11.58 | −£17,674 |
  Break-even: new buyer must cost **≤ £23** → with £6 per sign-up, **at least ~26% of the waitlist must buy** (37% if nobody reorders).
- **Guarantees are affordable:** taste promise + first-jar refund at a 7.5% claim rate = £1.56 per order (5% → £1.04, 10% → £2.08).
- **Founding 20% off any pack** cuts the room for ads to ~£10 per order during launch week. Fine for the warm waitlist (no new ad cost), loss-making on cold traffic: keep it for the list only.
- **Cash.** Startup £2,058 (50 jars £208, lab test ~£350 est., waitlist ads ~£1,500 est.). If cost per buyer stays ~£30, year 1 needs ~£10k more. At 30% list conversion (£20 per buyer) year 1 is just positive (+£1,226) but the startup is not earned back; at 50% (£12) the startup is earned back in month 6 and year 1 makes +£8,786.
- **Three ways to improve the margin:**
  1. More buyers from the list instead of cold ads (each list buyer costs ~£0 more): the email sequence before launch is the cheapest lever.
