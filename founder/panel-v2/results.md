## What would flip a no

- A tablet version, or a cheaper way to try one jar with free delivery and a proper trial.
- A sugar-free or much lower-sugar magnesium option at a Boots-style price, or a 3-for-2 type offer.
- Seeing the published lab results for the batch and a clear sugar figure per serving, so I can judge it myself before committing.
- A small trial pack, or a tablet version, so I could find out how it tastes before spending on a jar.
- A proper discount or a 3-for-2 style offer on the jar, or a clear comparison showing the gummy absorbs better than the Boots tablet.
- A low-sugar or sugar-free version, or a clear sugar figure per gummy I could compare with a normal tablet, plus proof the magnesium is what my body actually needs.
- A real discount, or a lower sugar count per serving that I can see on the jar, plus the published lab results convincing me it's the real dose.
- A proper price comparison with Boots or a supermarket magnesium, or a single-jar trial at a price close to that.
- A clear, checkable reason the citrate gummy is worth around five times a Boots tub, and a price for one jar closer to £10.
- A much lower price, close to the Boots price, or a free or cheap single jar to try before committing to more.
- Independent reviews from women my age who've been taking them for a month or two, showing they felt less tired, at a price nearer what I pay for my Boots vitamins.
- A few real reviews from women my age who've used it for at least a month and felt a difference.
