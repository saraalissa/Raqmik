# Mëva (trymeva.co) — CRO audit, 13 November 2026

Audit only. Nothing on the site was changed for this audit.
Method: automated walk-through of the live site in headless Chromium, iPhone 13 emulation (390px) and desktop (1366px), plus the Shopify admin API. Test traffic came from a US datacentre IP, not a UK phone on 4G.

---

## 1. Verdict

**Will convert with fixes. Not as it stands today.**

The message and offer are stronger than most pages in the category. Three faults lose sign-ups before a visitor reaches the form: the planned ad destination returns 404, the mobile first screen shows no headline or button, and the sign-up has never been proven to complete.

Confidence: medium to high. The one thing not proven either way is whether a real visitor's sign-up completes (see 4.4).

## 4. Findings in detail

### 4.1 The planned ad destination returns 404 (critical)

`/pages/offer` returns "Page not found" on mobile and desktop. The brief sends Meta traffic there. Any ad pointed at it today wastes every click.

Decision needed from the owner, one of:
- republish `/pages/offer` and use it as the single ad landing page, or
- point ads at `/products/meva-magnesium-gummies`.

Do not run both. The competitor evidence in the owner's own research supports one conversion page with angles tested in the ads.

### 4.2 Mobile first screen has no headline and no button

On a 390px screen the hero image of the jar fills the whole first view. Visible text: the announcement ticker, the logo, the "20%" seal and the eyebrow "For the women who hold everyone together". The H1 starts at the very bottom edge and is cut off. No CTA is visible without scrolling.

A cold visitor cannot answer "what is this, who is it for, what do I do" in 5 seconds on mobile. On desktop the hero passes the 5-second test.

### 4.3 The pop-up opens at 1.5 seconds

The waitlist pop-up opens 1.5 seconds after load on every page, including the 404 page. It covers the screen before the visitor has read the headline. It asks for an email from someone who does not yet know what the product is.

Behaviour that works well and should be kept: one email field on mobile, a clear close button and "Not now, thanks", hidden for 3 days after closing, never shown again after joining.

### 4.4 The sign-up has never been proven to work

- Hero CTA links to `#club`, which scrolls to the form. Good.
- Form fields: email (required) and "What's draining you most right now?" (optional).
- On submit, an hCaptcha challenge appeared and the page did not navigate. The test email was not created.
- The test browser is automated, so a challenge is expected and does not prove real visitors are blocked.
- **Shopify shows 0 customers.** Nobody has completed a sign-up yet, so there is no evidence the flow completes for anyone.

Not verified, because the test could not get past the captcha:
- whether the "You're in / Welcome to the No Drama Club" message shows
- whether any confirmation or welcome email is sent
- whether the customer is tagged `waitlist, founding-500`

### 4.5 Buttons and wording that imply buying today

The site is in waitlist mode but several elements read as a shop:
- product page sticky bar: "£39.98 · 2 jars · free delivery" with "Reserve mine"
- product page button: "Reserve mine · £39.98"
- a basket icon in the header on every page
- desktop header button: "Shop now"

A visitor may think she is about to pay £39.98. The honest message is "nothing to pay today".

|---|---|---|---|---|---|

| Product page | mobile | 0.9s | 1.4s | 2.9MB | 276 |

The product and why-meva figures were taken after the homepage in the same browser, so shared files were cached and their times are flattering. The request counts and page weights are high for mobile. LCP was not captured. Run PageSpeed Insights on mobile for both the homepage and the chosen ad landing page before spending on ads.

### 4.7 Page length

Homepage: 17,152px tall on mobile, about 1,560 words. Product page: 15,817px, about 1,600 words. Long pages are normal for this category, but only if a button is always close. See 4.2.

### 4.8 Tracking

- Meta Pixel is installed (ID 1647192600272885).
- Microsoft Clarity loads only after the visitor accepts analytics cookies. Tested: declined = no Clarity requests; accepted = Clarity loads.
- No Meta conversion is set up for a waitlist sign-up yet. Without it, Meta cannot optimise for sign-ups.

## 6. Compliance notes

No forbidden claims were found. A text scan of all three live pages for menopause, sleep, cure, clinically, doctor, best seller, most popular, as seen, reviews and customers returned nothing on the pages.

Three wording issues:

1. **Ticker: "30-day guarantee".** The guarantee covers the first jar of the first order only. More accurate: "30-day first-jar guarantee".
2. **Comparison table: "Balances your hormones".** It appears in "the usual hype" column as an example of what others claim, not as a Mëva claim. Low risk, but it puts hormone language on a page aimed at women 45–60. Safer to swap it for an example with no hormone wording.
3. **"The food gap" headline (unpublished theme): "It's not just you. It's the food."** It can be read as saying the visitor's tiredness is caused by low magnesium intake. The owner chose this wording and marked it locked. Flagged, not changed.

Statistics checked against the source (NDNS 2019–2023, Table 5.41, women 19–64, food sources only):
- 15% below the Lower Reference Nutrient Intake: correct.
- Average intake as % of the RNI: **83%**, not 80%. Any "~80%" figure should read 83% with this source.

## 7. Expected result with cold Meta traffic

These are estimates, not measurements.

- Typical range for a pre-launch waitlist page with a discount, cold traffic: 5–15% sign-up rate.
- Site as it stands: **2–5%**, assuming ads land on a page that exists and the form works for real visitors.
- After the fixes in section 8: **6–10%**.
- If ads point at `/pages/offer` today: **0%**.

### The three biggest leaks

1. **Ad destination.** `/pages/offer` is 404.
2. **Mobile first screen plus the early pop-up.** The visitor is asked for an email before she knows what the product is.
3. **Unproven sign-up.** Zero completed sign-ups in Shopify; captcha behaviour and the confirmation step are untested with a real visitor.

---

## 8. Fixes, in order

### Before any ad spend
1. **Choose the ad landing page** (4.1). Owner's decision.
2. **Complete one real sign-up** from a UK phone with a real email. Confirm three things: the "You're in" message appears, an email arrives, and the customer shows in Shopify with the tags `waitlist, founding-500`.
3. **Create the Meta custom conversion** "Waitlist sign-up": URL contains `customer_posted=true`. Confirm the test sign-up from step 2 registers in Events Manager.
4. **Publish the unpublished theme** so the stickers leave why-meva and "The food gap" appears on the product page.

### High impact, small effort
5. **Mobile hero:** shrink the image so the full H1 and one "Join the No Drama Club" button sit in the first screen.
6. **Pop-up timing:** open after 15–20 seconds or after 50% scroll, not at 1.5 seconds. Do not show it on the 404 page.
7. **Waitlist wording:** remove the price from "Reserve mine · £39.98", add "nothing to pay today" beside reserve buttons, change desktop "Shop now" to "Join the club", hide the basket icon while in waitlist mode.
8. **Ticker:** "30-day first-jar guarantee".

### After those
9. Run PageSpeed Insights on mobile and act on the largest items (image weight first).
10. Add a short welcome email if step 2 shows none is sent.
11. Privacy policy: add a line naming Meta Pixel and Microsoft Clarity.

### After the first sales
12. Real reviews through Judge.me, not incentivised. Real behaviour statistics from a post-purchase survey.

## 9. Hard constraints for any change

- Only these five claims, word for word: magnesium contributes to the reduction of tiredness and fatigue; to normal psychological function; to the normal functioning of the nervous system; to normal muscle function; to the maintenance of normal bones.
- Nothing implying menopause relief, hormone balance, sleep benefits, or cure/treat language.
- No invented reviews, ratings, customer counts or testimonials. No "most popular" or "best seller".
- No timed-result promises. Timelines describe the routine, not outcomes.
- Statistics must be real and sourced on the page.
- British English. Voice: the honest best friend with no drama; mature, not cutesy.
- Flavour wording: "ripe red berry".
- Do not expose the owner's personal email, phone or address anywhere customer-facing.
- Show a preview before anything goes live. The owner publishes.
