The Label
Cheat Sheet

How to read any supplement label in 30 seconds. Six things to check, in the order your eyes should find them. Works for any brand, ours included.

1

Find the actual amount of the nutrient

The big number on the front is sometimes the whole compound, not the nutrient itself. Look for the line that names the nutrient on its own, usually in the nutrition table.

“Magnesium citrate 1,000mg” is not 1,000mg of magnesium. Look for “Magnesium 180mg”.
2

Check the serving size

Is that amount per tablet, per gummy or per daily serving? A dose split across two, three or four pieces changes what one piece gives you.

“Per 2 gummies” means one gummy gives half.
3

Look at the %NRV

NRV is the Nutrient Reference Value: the daily amount used on UK labels. %NRV tells you how much of that one serving covers. For magnesium, the NRV is 375mg.

180mg magnesium = 48% NRV.
4

See which form is named

Minerals come in different forms (citrate, oxide, glycinate and more). A clear label names the form and gives one amount. If it’s a blend of several forms with only one total, you can’t tell how much of each you’re getting.

5

Read the claims word for word

In the UK, a vitamin or mineral can only make authorised health claims, in set wording. If a claim sounds bigger than that, be curious. More on page 2.

6

Scan the ingredients and the small print

Ingredients are listed by weight, biggest first. Allergens are highlighted, often in bold. Check the warnings, the best-before date and the UK business name and address.

Claims: what’s normal, what’s a red flag

✓ Sounds like an authorised claim

  • “Magnesium contributes to a reduction of tiredness and fatigue.”
  • “…contributes to normal muscle function.”
  • Plain, specific, a bit dry. That’s usually a good sign.

✕ Worth a second look

  • “Cures”, “treats”, “prevents” a condition
  • “Balances your hormones” or “detoxes”
  • “Miracle”, “guaranteed results”, “instant”
  • Big promises with no nutrient or amount named

The 30-second check

  1. What’s the actual amount of the nutrient?
  2. Per how many pieces?
  3. What %NRV is that?
  4. Which form, and is it named?
  5. Do the claims match the plain, authorised style?
  6. Anything in the ingredients or warnings I need to know?
